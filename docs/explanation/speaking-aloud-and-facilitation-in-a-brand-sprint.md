# Speaking aloud, typing, and who is in the room

The brand-sprint skill invites people to answer out loud, and supports a human facilitator leading a
founder or team. This page sets out what research says about two hypotheses behind that:

1. Speaking, through speech to text, is better than typing in a chat with AI.
2. Conversation between people is better than conversation with AI for authentic and intentional
   outcomes.

"Authentic" and "intentional" are not measures the research uses. The nearest ones are how much
people say, how personal and informative it is, how precise and candid it is, how well they learn
or remember, how original the ideas are, and how much people feel they own and commit to a decision.
Those point in different directions, so the findings below are grouped by what they measure.

## In short

* **Explaining improves thinking, but the voice is not what does it.** Being asked to explain is the
  active ingredient. Speech lowers the cost of explaining, so people say more, and more of it is
  personal. Typing gives more precise, more considered and often more candid answers.
* **People deciding together builds commitment and a shared view; AI adds sameness and agreement.**
  But groups that talk freely produce fewer ideas than people working alone, a person with AI
  matched a team without it in one large field study, and people disclose more to a machine.
* **Neither hypothesis has been tested directly.** No study found compares dictating with typing into
  the same AI chat, or a human-facilitated strategy workshop with an AI-facilitated one. Both are
  reasonable hypotheses with indirect support, not findings.
* **The design the evidence supports is mixed:** think and generate alone (speaking is fine and often
  richer), confirm decisions in writing, and decide together as people, with the AI structuring the
  work and pushing back rather than supplying the answers.

## Speaking compared with typing

### For

* **Explaining helps.** Prompting people to explain material to themselves improves learning: a
  meta-analysis of 64 reports and 5,917 participants found g = 0.55
  ([Bisra et al. 2018](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2018-bisra.pdf)). The original studies had people
  explain out loud ([Chi et al. 1994](https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/courses/cogscilearning/chiselfexplanation.pdf)).
* **Typing seems to cut explaining.** When students typed their self-explanations instead of
  speaking them, they produced significantly fewer, and more prompting partly made up for it
  (Hausmann and Chi 2002, as reported in
  [Chi's review](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/instruction_based_on_self_explanation.pdf);
  one small study, read secondhand).
* **Spoken answers are longer and more personal.** In three randomized trials with 7,766 answers,
  spoken answers ran about 14 words longer than typed ones (typed mean 17), were 20% more informative,
  and used more first-person experience and more nuanced positions
  ([Galasso, Nannicini and Nozza 2024](https://docs.iza.org/dp17488.pdf)). In a German survey, oral
  answers were up to about 40% longer, more varied and named more topics per answer; the authors read
  them as more spontaneous and intuitive, without testing that
  ([Gavras et al. 2022](https://core.ac.uk/download/491355938.pdf)).
* **Speech is about three times faster.** On phones, speech ran at 153 words a minute against 52 for
  the keyboard in English, with fewer corrected errors
  ([Ruan et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07323)). Less friction means people can say more.
* **Saying it aloud is remembered better.** Words read aloud are remembered better than words read
  silently, and hearing your own voice helps most
  ([MacLeod et al. 2010](https://uwaterloo.ca/memory-attention-cognition-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/jep10.pdf),
  [Forrin and MacLeod 2018](https://uwaterloo.ca/memory-attention-cognition-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/forrinmacleodmem18.pdf)).
  This is word-list memory, mostly within mixed lists, so it says little about reasoning.
* **Talking about stress did at least as well as writing about it.** In one small study, speaking
  gave the largest gains in coping and self-esteem, with writing in between
  ([Esterling et al. 1994](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Emotional-Disclosure-Through-Writing-or-Speaking/991031561750302976)).

### Against

* **Speaking alone changes nothing.** Across 94 studies, simply thinking aloud did not change
  performance (r = -.03); being asked to explain or justify did
  ([Fox, Ericsson and Best 2011](https://ifp.nyu.edu/?p=68982)). The question matters more than
  the mode.
* **Typing gave more precise and more candid answers.** In 634 randomized smartphone interviews, text
  answers were rounded less, more differentiated, and disclosed more sensitive behavior than voice
  answers ([Schober et al. 2015](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0128337)). With an AI chatbot
  over four weeks, the text condition showed the most self-disclosure, though its voice conditions also
  had the AI speak back ([Fang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473)).
* **Many people avoid voice.** Offered a choice, about 20% answered by voice, and every group offered
  voice responded less often than text alone
  ([Revilla and Couper 2026](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/srm/article/view/8456/7885)). Dropout was
  45% for voice against 13% for text in Gavras et al., and completion 65% against 98% in Galasso et al.
* **Writing helps precision and integration.** Written recall beat spoken recall
  ([Fueller, Loescher and Indefrey 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3798867/)), and
  composing in writing reshapes what the writer knows
  ([Kellogg 2008](https://jowr.org/index.php/jowr/article/view/759)). Most studies of expressive
  writing against talking into a recorder find comparable effects
  ([Pennebaker 1997](https://sanlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2016/03/Pennebaker_Psychscience_1997.pdf)).
* **Putting things into words can hurt insight and visual judgment.** Describing a face lowered later
  recognition ([Alogna et al. 2014](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/65957)), and verbalizing during insight
  problems cut solutions from about 46% to 36% ([Schooler, Ohlsson and Brooks 1993](https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/schooler/jonathan/node/1376)).

### Gaps

No controlled study compares dictating with typing into the same AI chat for depth or reflection.
Researchers have proposed exactly this hypothesis for writing with AI, without data yet
([Kim et al. 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.in2writing-1.7.pdf)). The survey evidence is the
closest, and its voice samples are self-selected.

## People compared with AI

### For

* **A trusted listener changes what the speaker believes.** Tailoring what you say to an audience
  shifts your own later memory and belief, but only when you want a shared view with a listener you
  trust and feel connected to ([Echterhoff, Higgins and Levine 2009](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/attitudesandsocialjudgment/echterhoffhigginslevine_sharedreality_pps2009.pdf)).
  This is how a team comes to hold a brand in common. Whether an AI can be that listener is untested.
  The classic studies used written messages, so the listener matters more than the voice.
* **People commit to what they build and decide.** People value what they made themselves, when they
  finish it ([Norton, Mochon and Ariely 2012](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/861385)), and
  commit to decisions they disagree with when the process engaged them and explained itself
  ([Kim and Mauborgne, Fair Process](https://ieanea.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/FairProcessArticle.pdf)).
* **People feel less ownership of AI-written text.** Writers did not feel they owned text the AI
  generated, and ownership rose with their influence over it
  ([Draxler et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03283)). A small preprint found the lowest
  ownership and recall among essay writers using an LLM
  ([Kosmyna et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872)).
* **AI agrees and converges.** Assistants tend to match the user's view over the truth
  ([Sharma et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)), which is the opposite of a facilitator's
  push. AI-assisted ideas are rated more creative one by one but are more alike across people
  ([Doshi and Hauser 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00506)); ChatGPT-assisted idea sets were less
  diverse in 37 of 45 comparisons ([Meincke, Nave and Terwiesch 2025](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/does-ai-limit-our-creativity)).
  For a brand, whose point is to be distinct, sameness is a real cost.
* **A heard voice seems more thoughtful.** Listeners judged a speaker more intelligent and more human
  when they heard the words than when they read them
  ([Schroeder and Epley 2015](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c171ac1710699e060ed3d94/t/5c7b005d8165f5e18075f621/1551564894483/the-sound-of-intellect.pdf),
  [Schroeder, Kardas and Epley 2017](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4bd9d03k)). Speech to text
  removes this; a room of people keeps it.

### Against

* **Free group talk loses ideas.** People brainstorming alone produce more ideas than the same number
  talking together, mostly because only one person can speak at a time
  ([Diehl and Stroebe 1987](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)).
  A meta-analysis found the loss in quality as well as quantity, larger in bigger groups and when ideas
  were spoken rather than written ([Mullen, Johnson and Salas 1991](https://www.scilit.net/publications/e7218c55746018a0c3a5fc3d0a54871a)).
  Alternating solo writing with group review gave 71% more ideas per person per minute
  (Paulus et al. 2015, as [summarized by APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/minds-business/theres-a-better-way-to-brainstorm.html)).
* **A person with AI matched a team without it.** In a field experiment with 776 professionals at
  Procter and Gamble, individuals with AI improved solution quality by 0.37 standard deviations, teams
  with AI by 0.39 and teams without AI by 0.24; only teams with AI were about three times as likely to
  reach the top tenth, and AI raised positive emotion
  ([Dell'Acqua et al. 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33641)). The best results came from people
  together with AI, not people instead of AI. It measured quality, not ownership.
* **People are more candid with a machine.** People who believed a virtual interviewer was automated
  feared disclosure less and managed impressions less than those told a person ran it
  ([Lucas et al. 2014](https://ict.usc.edu/pubs/It%e2%80%99s%20only%20a%20computer%20-%20The%20impact%20of%20human-agent%20interaction%20in%20clinical%20interviews.pdf), linked through its companion conference paper). Automated interviews also drew more
  disclosure in Schober et al.

### Gaps

No study found compares a human-facilitated brand or strategy workshop with an AI-facilitated one on
commitment, authenticity or follow-through. The ownership studies are about writing, not group
decisions.

## What this means for the brand-sprint skill

* **Keep inviting speech, and keep it optional.** It draws out more, and more personal, material,
  and suits the founder talking it through alone. Many people prefer typing, so neither mode is
  required.
* **Ask people to explain, not just answer.** The gain comes from the demand to explain. "Why does
  that matter?", "what would you give up to keep it?" and "say it as you would to a customer" do more
  than the microphone does.
* **Speak to explore, confirm in writing.** The skill's tight playback of each decision, for the
  person to confirm or correct, is the precise, deliberate step that typing provides. Keep it.
* **In a workshop, everyone writes alone before anyone talks, then the room decides together.**
  Writing first avoids losing ideas to turn-taking; talking and deciding together is where the shared
  view and commitment come from.
* **The AI structures and challenges; it does not supply the brand.** Ask for the person's answer
  before offering examples, label examples, and never draft values or positioning for them. This
  guards against sameness, agreement and lost ownership.
* **Place before describing.** For the personality sliders and the competitive map, have people mark
  positions first and explain after, since putting a visual judgment into words can bend it.
* **The facilitator relays the room's words, not their own.** A trusted listener shifts what the
  speaker comes to believe, which helps a team align and can also carry a facilitator's framing into
  the brand.
* **Treat the two hypotheses as open.** They have indirect support and no direct test. If it comes to
  matter, record whether a sprint was spoken or typed, solo or facilitated, and compare what came of
  it.
