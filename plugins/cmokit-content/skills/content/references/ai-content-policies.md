# Platform rules on AI-made content

What each platform says about content made or changed with AI, with where each rule comes from and
how sure it is. The content skill reads this file before a piece is drafted or handed off for
posting. A maintainer reviews every change to it; the monthly refresh proposes its changes as a pull
request, and `as_of` is the date of the last refresh that was merged.

Each entry has:

* `rule`: what the source says, in plain words, and what it does not say.
* `applies_to`: the asset levels below it concerns. `ads` marks a rule for paid ads only.
* `requirement`: `none`, `label`, `prohibited`, `reach_risk` (distribution may be limited) or `info`.
* `source_url` and `source_type`: `primary` (the platform's or regulator's own page), `news`,
  `vendor` or `unverified`.
* `evidence`: a short quote from the source, or a note on why there is none.
* `verified_on`: the date the source was last read, or last found unchanged since it was read.
  `none` means not yet.
* `status`: `current`, `needs_verification` or `unfetchable`.

## Asset levels

* **L0**: AI-assisted writing and planning (captions, scripts, ideas, scheduling).
* **L1**: minor AI edits to real media (retouching, captions, cutouts).
* **L2**: significant AI edits to real media (changes what a real person says or does, or their
  appearance).
* **L3**: fully AI-generated realistic image, audio or video.
* **L4**: AI-generated person or persona presented as a human or as an account identity.
* **L5**: automated engagement (AI-generated comments, DMs or likes at scale).
* **L6**: reposting other accounts' content.

## Instagram (Meta)

### IG-1
- rule: Profiles featuring an AI-generated person should turn on the "AI-generated profile" label. Unlabeled profiles may have their reach limited and may become ineligible for recommendations; profiles that add the label proactively see no change in reach. Announced 2026-08-31. The page does not define "AI-generated person" or explain detection.
- applies_to: L4
- requirement: reach_risk
- source_url: https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "We will also limit the reach of profiles that feature an AI-generated person and do not add this label."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### IG-2
- rule: Using AI tools as part of the creative process does not require the AI-generated profile label. This is about the profile label only, not about labels on posts.
- applies_to: L0, L1
- requirement: none
- source_url: https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "Creators who simply use AI tools as part of their creative process don't need to add the label."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### IG-3
- rule: Meta shows an "AI info" label on content it detects was generated by an AI tool, from industry-shared signals or from the creator's self-disclosure. Meta requires people to use its disclosure and label tool when they post organic content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and may apply penalties if they do not; it does not say what the penalties are. The current statement is Meta's 2026 midterms post (2026-02, updated 2026-06-01). Meta's Transparency Center page on labeling (updated 2025-02-19) and its 2024 newsroom post describe the label and self-disclosure but state no requirement or penalty for organic posts. No Instagram help-center article on the disclosure tool was found.
- applies_to: L2, L3
- requirement: label
- source_url: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/meta-prepares-for-2026-us-midterms/ ; https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content/ ; https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/ (2024, modified 2025-10-23)
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "We require people to use this disclosure and label tool when they post organic content with a photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and we may apply penalties if they fail to do so."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### IG-4
- rule: Accounts that regularly repost content they did not create are removed from recommendations (Reels, and now photos and carousels). People who follow such an account still see its posts. No numeric threshold is given, and AI is not mentioned. Meta's original announcement was not read.
- applies_to: L6
- requirement: reach_risk
- source_url: https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/
- source_type: news
- evidence: "Instagram announced on Thursday that accounts that regularly repost content they didn't create, or primarily share other people's work as photos and carousels, will no longer be eligible for recommendations across the app."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### IG-5
- rule: Ads about social issues, elections or politics must disclose a photorealistic image or video, or realistic-sounding audio, created or edited with third-party generative AI tools to show a real person saying or doing something they did not, a realistic person or event that does not exist or did not happen, altered footage of a real event, or a realistic event that is not a true recording. Undisclosed ads are rejected, and repeated failure may bring penalties against the advertiser. From 2026-06-01 Meta also detects such media automatically. Applies to ads only, not organic posts.
- applies_to: ads
- requirement: label
- source_url: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/SIEP-advertising/SIEP/
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "If we determine that an advertiser has not disclosed as required, we will reject the ad, and repeated failure to disclose may result in penalties against the advertiser."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

## TikTok

### TT-1
- rule: Creators must label AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video. For other content completely generated or significantly edited by AI (real footage changed beyond minor corrections, such as a subject shown doing or saying something they did not, or made unrecognizable), TikTok says it encourages a label rather than requiring one. Unlabeled realistic AI content may be removed, restricted or labeled by TikTok.
- applies_to: L2, L3
- requirement: label
- source_url: https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "We also require creators to label all AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio, and video, as explained in our Community Guidelines."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### TT-2
- rule: TikTok labels content made with its own AI effects, or carrying C2PA Content Credentials, automatically, and a creator cannot remove an automatic label. AI edits made outside TikTok's effects still need the creator's own label.
- applies_to: L2, L3
- requirement: info
- source_url: https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "Note: Once your content is labeled as AI-generated with an auto label, you won't be able to remove the label from your post."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### TT-3
- rule: Prohibited whatever the label: fake authoritative sources or crisis events; false depictions of public figures in certain contexts, such as bullying or endorsements; and the likeness of anyone under 18, or of a private adult without their permission. The list is on TikTok's support page; the Community Guidelines say only that labeled AI content can still be harmful.
- applies_to: L3, L4
- requirement: prohibited
- source_url: https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content ; https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "Some AI-generated content or edited media can still cause harm, even if properly labeled."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### TT-4
- rule: Viewers can turn AI-generated content up or down in Settings, Content Preferences, Manage Topics (announced 2025-11-18), and TikTok is testing invisible watermarking. TikTok does not state any effect on creators' reach, so a reach effect is an inference, not a rule.
- applies_to: L2, L3
- requirement: reach_risk
- source_url: https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/
- source_type: news
- evidence: "You can access the new capability by going into Settings > Content Preferences > Manage Topics."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### TT-5
- rule: Turning on TikTok's AI-generated content label does not affect a video's distribution, as long as the video does not break the Community Guidelines. The page does not mention For You eligibility by name or address AI-written text.
- applies_to: L0, L2, L3
- requirement: info
- source_url: https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "Note: Turning on the AI-generated content setting won't affect the distribution of your video as long as it doesn't violate our Community Guidelines."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

## LinkedIn

### LI-1
- rule: No ranking penalty has been reported for AI-assisted writing. LinkedIn launched a "Seems like AI slop" flag (reported 2026-07-31), replaced its "enhance your post" tool with a proofreader, and is strengthening verification and bot detection; its product chief said AI use isn't always bad. The article does not say whether flagged posts lose reach.
- applies_to: L0
- requirement: none
- source_url: https://fortune.com/2026/07/31/linkedin-seems-like-ai-slop-button-billions-automated-comments-attempts/
- source_type: news
- evidence: "At the same time, he clarified that using AI isn't always bad, noting that many people refine their thoughts with AI"
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### LI-2
- rule: LinkedIn says it blocks hundreds of thousands of automated slop comment attempts every day.
- applies_to: L5
- requirement: prohibited
- source_url: https://fortune.com/2026/07/31/linkedin-seems-like-ai-slop-button-billions-automated-comments-attempts/
- source_type: news
- evidence: "Srinivasan said the company detects and blocks hundreds of thousands of automated slop comment attempts every day"

### LI-3
- rule: A vendor blog (depost.ai) claims LinkedIn limits the reach of AI-written posts, with 40% fewer views. Nothing confirms it; the 40% figure in Fortune is a detector company's estimate of how many posts are AI-written. Do not use this claim in advice.
- applies_to: L0
- requirement: reach_risk
- source_url: depost.ai (exact page not recorded)
- source_type: vendor
- evidence: none; the claim is recorded only so that it is not repeated.
- verified_on: none
- status: needs_verification

### LI-4
- rule: LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies forbid sharing synthetic or manipulated media that shows a person saying or doing something they did not, without clearly disclosing that it is fake or altered. Its help page on false or misleading content says LinkedIn removes synthetic or manipulated content that misrepresents or distorts real-life events without clear disclosure, generally requires members to disclose the fake or altered nature of such media, and may not require an explicit disclosure when context makes obvious parody or satire clear. Neither page mentions Content Credentials, so whether signed content shows an icon is not confirmed.
- applies_to: L2, L3
- requirement: label
- source_url: https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies ; https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340752
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "Do not share synthetic or manipulated media that depicts a person saying something they did not say or doing something they did not do without clearly disclosing the fake or altered nature of the material."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

### LI-5
- rule: LinkedIn's guidance on content created with AI recommends telling others, if it is not obvious from context, when you relied heavily on AI to create or change a post. It is advice, not a rule. The same page says LinkedIn judges whether content adds value, not how it was made: content that feels generic, repetitive or lacks a clear point of view is less likely to be widely distributed, and a "Seems like AI" tip in Post analytics means only that enough members flagged the post, not that it was taken down. The page does not mention Content Credentials.
- applies_to: L0
- requirement: info
- source_url: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1481496
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "In your posts or other content on LinkedIn, we recommend that you let others know (if it isn't obvious from the context) if you've relied heavily on AI to help create or modify the content."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

## EU (only when the audience includes people in the EU; never legal advice)

### EU-1
- rule: Article 50 of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) applies from 2026-08-02. A deployer of an AI system that generates or manipulates a deepfake image, audio or video must disclose that it was artificially generated or manipulated, clearly and at the latest at a person's first exposure, and the Commission says the provider's machine-readable marking alone does not meet that duty. For evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work the duty is only to disclose the content's existence in a way that does not hamper the work. AI-generated or manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be disclosed, unless it has had human review or editorial control and a person holds editorial responsibility for it. The Commission says purely personal use is outside the Act. Breaching Article 50 can bring fines of up to EUR 15 million or, for an undertaking, 3 percent of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (Article 99(4)). Content made before 2026-08-02 need not be labeled retroactively.
- applies_to: L2, L3
- requirement: label
- source_url: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj ; https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake, shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current

## General

### GEN-1
- rule: Keep C2PA Content Credentials and other metadata intact. TikTok reads Content Credentials on uploaded content to label it as AI-generated automatically. The page does not say whether labels survive re-uploading or editing elsewhere.
- applies_to: L1, L2, L3
- requirement: info
- source_url: https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content
- source_type: primary
- evidence: "Content Credentials attach metadata to content that we can use to recognize and label AIGC instantly."
- verified_on: 2026-11-06
- status: current
