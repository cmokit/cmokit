# Reels, carousels and Stories: the craft

Read the part a step names. Figures from platforms are cited with the date they were read; figures
from third parties are marked as theirs; starting points that are CMOkit's own are marked as ours.
The weekly review adjusts any starting point.

## Event patterns (step 1)

Plan around promotable things: a single event, a series, a format or a product. Each has an id, a
name, a pattern and a destination, and the id is the `utm_campaign` on every piece that promotes it.

| Pattern | How to spot it | What gets promoted | Content mix | Link route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `launch` | One date or offer that matters a lot: a big event, a product launch, an annual event | That event or offer | The backwards campaign below | Profile link to its page for the run; Story link to it |
| `series` | The same host and slot on a rhythm the audience can learn, even with skipped months or changing titles | The series and its rhythm | Evergreen pieces about the series, a reminder Story a few days before each session and on the day, a recap after. A new topic each session also gets a short campaign | Profile link to the calendar; Story link to the next session |
| `event` | One of a few different events in a period, each happening once | Each event, scaled to its size | Announce, one or two reasons to come, last call, staggered so lead pieces do not land on the same day | Story link to its page; profile link to the calendar |
| `format` | A kind of event repeated often, dates added as they come up | The format | The three layers below | Profile link to the calendar; Story link to each date |
| `product` | Something sold from a purchase page | The product | Like a format: evergreen pieces, the link in the profile, Stories for offers | Profile link to the purchase page |

Sorting (ours, from testing against real calendars):

* Sort from history, not from upcoming dates alone: the upcoming list hides series.
* The title is a weak signal; it changes by month, topic and rename. Group by host, weekday, time
  and venue, plus the title with dates, months and topics stripped.
* A group that repeats on a rough rhythm, allowing skipped months and the odd moved date, is a
  `series`. If every title differs, promote the slot as the series and give each topic a short
  campaign.
* Irregular dates with no rhythm make a `format`. A title back once a year is a `launch` that can
  reuse last year's photos and results. A one-time variant of a series (a special edition, another
  city) is an `event` that borrows the series' evergreen pieces.
* Keep old names as `aliases`. Mark anything not to promote with `promote: false`, such as
  members-only sessions or partner conferences on other sites.

### Three layers, for series and formats

| Layer | What | How often (ours) | Call to action and link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evergreen | Reels and carousels about the experience: spotlight, recap, what people said, behind the scenes | 2 to 3 a week, rotating across series and formats | "See upcoming dates, link in profile" |
| What's on this week | One Story set or a carousel listing the dates open in the next 7 days, saved to an "Upcoming" Highlight | Weekly, plus a Story when a date is added | Story link sticker to the calendar |
| Short-lead, per date | A Story when a date opens and the day before or of; a last-call photo if a date needs filling | As dates open and approach | Story link sticker to that event's page |

Rotate, do not repost: Instagram is less likely to recommend a Reel already posted (Instagram reach
tips). Share an older evergreen Reel to Stories instead; a new hook or new footage counts as a new
Reel.

### Campaigns, for launches and events

Plan backwards from the date. For an event: announce when registration opens, 2 to 3 spotlights
(speaker, room, what you will feel or learn), a last call 2 to 4 days out, and a recap 1 to 3 days
after with real footage that feeds the next one. For a product: a teaser, a spotlight on one real
detail per piece, social proof with consent, and a last call if the offer ends. Weight the end:
Eventbrite says nearly 40% of events sell less than half their registrations until the final days
(Eventbrite, for its own data).

Cadence (ours): 2 to 3 Reels a week in a campaign, a same-day Story for each to carry its link, and
about one carousel a week. Give each piece one job: be discovered, build desire, or ask for the
action.

## Link routes (steps 1 and 6)

| Route | Clickable | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Caption | No, plain text | Say where the link is |
| Profile link | Yes | Shared by every piece, so it credits whichever is current |
| Story link sticker | Yes, every account (Instagram, Oct 2021) | The default carrier for a piece's own tracked link |
| Comment keyword, link by DM | Yes, in the DM | Needs an automation tool the person already uses |

Default route: the caption says where to go, the profile link points at the current promotable
thing's page or the calendar, and a same-day Story's link sticker carries the piece's tracked link.

## Story structures (step 2)

A small, fixed set, so results can be compared:

* **Before, turn, after.** A real tension, the moment it shifts, how it feels now. Spotlights and
  recaps.
* **Three things.** A list of three, easy to film and to follow.
* **Behind the scenes.** Preparing the room, the product, the ritual. Teasers.
* **What people said.** A guest's or buyer's real words, with consent noted. Recaps and proof.
* **Countdown or last call.** The date, what you will miss, the link. Short.

Hooks: land it in the first few seconds. Lead with the viewer's benefit, say up front what the clip
shows, or ask a question that sparks curiosity (Meta, via Social Media Today, Dec 2025). Speech in the
first 3 seconds and a face early lifted retention in one study (Emplifi, third party).

Length: 45 to 60 seconds had the best median views in one 2026 study of brand Reels, and under 30
seconds less than half as many (Socialinsider, third party). Start at 20 to 45 seconds for a last call
and 45 to 60 for spotlights and recaps (ours).

Each layer has a job. On-screen text carries the hook and each beat's key line, one idea per screen.
A voice carries the feeling and the detail. Auto captions make speech readable with sound off. The
caption's first line repeats or answers the hook, its body adds plain searchable keywords, and it
names where the link is. One action per piece, with its reason and deadline, said in the video and
the caption.

Carousels: the first slide is the hook, one idea per slide, the last slide is the call to action,
plus "save this" when the detail is worth keeping. Up to 20 slides. Music only on all-photo carousels.

## Shot needs (step 3)

Each beat names its kind of footage: A-roll (a person speaking or doing the main action), B-roll
(detail and atmosphere over the words) or a still. Give the shot need in plain words ("hands, close
up, pouring"), good alternatives ("any close-up of hands or the cup"), and what to do with nothing
("a still of the room, or film 3 seconds of it empty"). For an upcoming shoot, give the same list
ahead of filming.

## Finishing: the Edits checklist (step 5)

* 9:16, 1080 by 1920.
* Safe zones: keep text and faces out of roughly the top 14% and bottom 35%, and away from the right
  edge (a third party's summary of Meta's guidance).
* Cover: the profile grid crops to 3:4, so keep the cover title and any face in the middle 1080 by
  1440. Carousel text also stays inside the 3:4 crop.
* Pacing (ours): cut on each beat, most beats 1.5 to 4 seconds, hold the hook long enough to read
  twice, one transition style, mostly straight cuts.
* Text style: the brand's font and two colours from its design system, set once in Edits.
* Captions on for any speech; check the words.
* Music: a mood and a few search words, kept under the voice, from music the account may use
  commercially. Business accounts get Meta's licensed library.
* Originality: no other app's watermark, no reposts, no borders, not mostly text.

## Alt text (step 6)

Instagram documents alt text for photos and for each carousel image, under Advanced settings while
posting, and under Edit, then Accessibility, after. It documents none for Reels or Stories, so a Reel's
accessibility is its captions and on-screen text. Keep each under 100 characters (ours, which fits
every limit found). Describe what is in the picture, not the brand's message.

## Measuring (step 7)

* Is the hook working? Skip rate and the first seconds of the retention chart.
* Is the story holding? Average watch time against length, and where retention drops.
* Will it spread? Shares and share rate; for carousels, saves and shares.
* Did it drive the action? Profile visits and link sticker taps, then the registrations
  `results_read` counts.

## Default AI tells

A starting list for the `ai_tells` section; the person adds and removes.

* Words: delve, elevate, unlock, unleash, seamless, game-changer, journey (when not literal),
  tapestry, testament, vibrant, curated, embark.
* Openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "Ready to...?", "In
  conclusion".
* Shapes: "It's not just X, it's Y"; three adjectives in a row; every list in threes; a question
  answered in the next line; emoji as bullet points; dashes used as commas.
* Claims with no source: "studies show", "experts agree".
